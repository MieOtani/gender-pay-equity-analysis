# People Analytics Case Study: Gender Pay Equity Analysis

## Project Overview

This project presents a People Analytics consulting engagement conducted for **Splash Technologies** to evaluate gender pay equity across departments and assess whether compensation is aligned with employee performance.

Using workforce data extracted from the company's HRIS, the objective was to identify compensation disparities, validate a business hypothesis through data analysis, and provide evidence-based recommendations to support fair and sustainable compensation practices.

---

## Business Problem

Although Splash Technologies demonstrated strong representation of women in leadership positions, leadership suspected that compensation equity might not be consistent across the organization.

The objective was to determine:

- Whether gender pay disparities existed across departments.
- Whether high-performing employees were rewarded consistently regardless of gender.
- Which areas required immediate HR intervention.

---

## Business Hypothesis

> Gender-based pay differences are department-specific rather than company-wide, with the IT/IS department presenting the largest compensation gap, particularly among high-performing female employees.

The analysis validated this hypothesis.

---

## Methodology

The project followed a structured People Analytics workflow from raw HRIS data to executive recommendations.

### 1. Data Preparation

The raw workforce dataset extracted from the HRIS was cleaned and standardized by:

- Correcting inconsistent values
- Standardizing department and position identifiers
- Handling missing data
- Validating salary information
- Preparing the dataset for reliable analysis

---

### 2. Exploratory Data Analysis

After cleaning the data, the workforce population was reviewed to identify:

- Potential compensation inconsistencies
- Department-level salary patterns
- Performance rating distributions
- Gender representation across functions

These findings were used to define the business problem and formulate the analytical hypothesis.

---

### 3. Workforce Analysis

Using Microsoft Excel, multiple analyses were performed, including:

- Gender pay gap calculations
- Department-level salary comparisons
- Performance vs. compensation analysis
- Descriptive statistics
- Comparative workforce metrics

---

### 4. Executive Reporting

The analytical findings were translated into an executive presentation summarizing:

- Key insights
- Business risks
- Evidence supporting the hypothesis
- Recommended HR interventions
- Long-term People Analytics strategy

## Key Findings

The analysis identified that compensation inequity was concentrated within a specific business function rather than across the entire organization.

Major findings included:

- Average gender pay gap of **4.9%**
- IT/IS presented the largest departmental pay disparity
- Female employees rated **"Exceeds Expectations"** earned approximately **46.7% less** than male employees with the same rating within IT/IS
- Compensation was not consistently aligned with employee performance
- High-performing female employees were, in some cases, paid less than female employees with lower performance ratings

These findings suggested a compensation governance issue rather than isolated salary anomalies.

---

## Business Recommendations

### Immediate Actions

- Conduct a targeted compensation audit within IT/IS
- Review performance calibration processes
- Implement exception reporting for pay progression by gender and performance

### Strategic Recommendations

- Introduce pay equity metrics into HR dashboards
- Provide bias-awareness training for people leaders
- Align compensation bands with market benchmarks and internal compa-ratio targets
- Establish ongoing monitoring of compensation equity through People Analytics
